# Civic-Chain × Broadband Telecom: A Partnership Proposal

**Created:** 2026-10-14. **Prepared for:** Broadband Telecom Services. **From:** Nick Hernandez, Civic-Chain.

What this is. You help Virginia towns win grants. We help them prove where the money went. This is a proposal to turn the municipal and grant relationships Broadband Telecom already owns into a recurring revenue stream, by co-selling Civic-Chain's public-money accountability platform into the towns and agencies you already serve.

The offer. **10% of the net profit Civic-Chain earns on every deal you source, for the full life of the contract.** You open the door and co-sell. We build, deliver, and support. The share is recurring, and it grows as each town climbs to the next product.

## 1. Why Civic-Chain, and why now

Every town you serve moves public money, contracts, grants, vendor payments, and almost none of it can be independently verified. Residents are handed portals full of numbers no one can audit. Auditors reconcile by hand, months later. And federal programs now demand proof that every grant dollar was spent as promised.

- **The problem.** Towns move billions through contracts and grant programs on open-data portals that publish figures no one can verify and spreadsheets no one can audit. When a grant is questioned or a payment goes wrong, there is no single record that proves what actually happened.
- **The product.** Civic-Chain is one verifiable record per public contract: a public, tamper-evident ledger residents and auditors can query in plain English, spend cards that only pay a vendor after work is confirmed, and AI that reconciles every figure to the books or refuses to answer.
- **The timing.** Federal programs, BEAD broadband, IIJA, ARPA, and improper-payment laws now require towns to prove accountability for every public dollar. The buyers you already serve are being told to do exactly what our platform does.

Three federal shifts raise the stakes right now: **$162B** in improper federal payments in fiscal 2024 across 68 programs, with the federal auditor saying the real figure is likely higher; a **December 31, 2026** deadline to spend ARPA recovery money, with reporting running until Treasury closes each award; and a **$1M-a-year** threshold that now triggers a full Single Audit, for fiscal years starting October 1, 2024 or later. Towns are asked to prove more, on tighter deadlines, with the same small staff. Sources: GAO-25-107753 (March 2025); U.S. Treasury SLFRF Final Rule via UNC School of Government (January 2026); 2 CFR 200.501.

## 2. The product your towns would be buying

Three products on one engine, sold as a ladder: a town starts with the cheap, easy wedge and expands up over time. Because the engine is schema-agnostic, every new product is a new allow-list, not new plumbing.

| Stage | Product | What it does | What it replaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Land, the wedge** | **TLaaS, the Transparency Layer** | A public, tamper-evident record of every public dollar, queryable in plain English, with unlimited users and every figure checked against the books. | Legacy open-data portals (Socrata, OpenGov) at roughly a tenth of the cost |
| **Expand, the prize** | **DirectRail, vendor payments + spend cards** | Vendor payments on locked cards, released only after a milestone is confirmed, with duplicate and over-contract detection. DirectRail pays vendors in dollars at one flat fee per payment, and every payment lands on the same record. | Enterprise contract software and the 2.25 to 2.75% incumbent payment fees we undercut |
| **Attach, the upsell** | **Verified Intelligence** | Ask about the books in plain English; every figure is reconciled to the data or the system refuses to answer, re-runnable to the identical result. | The annual audit and compliance software |

TLaaS is ready today. DirectRail is built and in testing; no town pays this way yet, and the law has not been tested on it. The subscription starts small and recurring; the payment product adds a small per-payment fee once the town runs spend through our rails. This is recurring, high-margin software and payments, not one-time services, which is exactly why a profit share on it is worth having.

**Why there is an opening.** Today a town either reports late or pays a cut, and nobody keeps one current record of the money and moves it at a flat cost.

| What towns use today | What it does | What it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|
| Reports built by hand | Quick to read | Out of date the moment they are finished |
| Budget and transparency dashboards | Show the public a picture of the budget | Updated on a schedule, apart from how the money actually moves |
| Card payments | Move the money | Card networks keep 1.8% to 2.8% of every payment |
| Government payment processors | Take resident and vendor payments | Typical government processing runs 2.25% to 2.75% on ACH |

## 5. The partnership: 10% of net profit on every deal you source

The structure is deliberately simple. You do what you are already good at, opening doors in the public sector, and you share in the profit of everything that follows.

- **What you bring.** Warm introductions and co-selling into the municipal and grant relationships you already have. *No build. No support. No capital.*
- **What you earn.** 10% of the net profit Civic-Chain earns on every contract you originate, for the full life of that contract, including renewals and every product the town adds up the ladder. *Recurring, not a one-time referral fee.*
- **How it's paid.** Attribution is agreed in writing when a deal is registered to you; the share is paid on recognized, collected revenue, on a regular schedule. *Transparent and auditable, the same standard our product holds everyone to.*

### Why the 10% is real money

- Civic-Chain is a **high-margin, recurring business**: subscription software plus a small fee on the payments a town runs through our rails. The software margins are high and the revenue renews every year.
- **"Net profit on a deal you source"** means the revenue recognized from that account minus the direct costs to deliver and run it, hosting, payment-rail, and support. It is not a slice of gross revenue and not a one-time bounty; it is a standing share of the profit that account produces for as long as it stays with us.
- Because governments rarely switch vendors and towns **climb from the wedge to the payment product over time**, the profit on each relationship, and your share of it, grows year over year without you selling again.
- Exact definitions, attribution windows, and payment terms are set in a short written partner agreement. This page is the offer, not the contract.

## 10. How a Virginia town can buy

A first-year pilot does not need a sealed bid. Virginia's small-purchase rules give a town a clean, fast path to start:

| Ceiling | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **$200,000** | Goods and non-professional services, single or term contracts, without sealed bids or formal negotiation. |
| **$80,000** | The separate, lower ceiling for professional services such as legal or engineering work. |
| **Total contract value** | Counts toward the ceiling, not one year, so a one-year pilot keeps it small. |

Each locality must adopt its own written procurement procedures and may set lower limits, and competition is still required where practicable. We check each town's policy first. Source: Code of Virginia § 2.2-4303(G).

## 11. Trust and security

Because Civic-Chain handles public money, every procurement review asks the same hard questions. We answer them in one place, in plain language, at [civic-chain.com/trust](https://civic-chain.com/trust), with the documents a review needs. Where a claim is still in progress, we say so.

- **Software, not a bank.** Civic-Chain is procurement and payment-orchestration software. Money settles through licensed partners into a vendor's bank account. We make no money-transmitter-license claims.
- **Two rails, kept separate.** Public money moves on one rail. The public record moves on another. They meet only at a single public ledger relay, and they meet only as records. Not one cent of a town's money moves on-chain.
- **What goes on the ledger, and what stays sealed.** A confirmed write anchors a cryptographic hash of the record, a budget or warrant code, the amount, and the hash of the record before it. Payee names, bank account numbers, transaction memos, and any personal detail of a resident or vendor stay sealed off-chain. The ledger is chain-agnostic; we say public ledger, and we do not tie a town to one network.
- **SOC 2 readiness, data handling, and uptime.** We run a SOC 2 readiness program and will not imply a report exists before one is issued. Our subprocessor list names every vendor that can touch covered data and is kept current, under our Data Processing Addendum (DPA). Uptime targets and service credits are defined in our Service Level Agreement (SLA).

The full review packet, security overview, subprocessor list, SLA, regulatory memo, and the reconciled Privacy Policy and Terms, is available to a town's legal and procurement teams, under NDA where required.

Most referral deals pay once and the vendor disappears after the sale. This one pays every year the town stays.

| Usually | With Civic-Chain |
|---|---|
| A one-time referral fee | A share every year the town stays |
| The vendor is gone after the sale | We run and support the town for the life of the contract |
| Grant records pulled together by hand at closeout | One current record, all year |
| Each new product is a new sale | Every product the town adds raises your share, with no new sale |

And the model only works when the town wins. A current record means easier audits and lower fees for the town, residents see where the money went, you earn recurring income on relationships you already have, and Civic-Chain gets a trusted way into towns we have no footprint in. Nobody wins unless the towns do. That is why the share runs for the life of the contract.
